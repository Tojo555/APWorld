Read this first: what a strong answer looks like

Example prompt (Question 1, 2 pts): How was the Russian empire similar to and different from those of the Spanish and British?
A POOR ANSWER “The Russian empire had a tax native peoples had to pay in furs. The British were a maritime empire.”
Why it’s poor: it isn’t comparing or contrasting related things. Each sentence is about a different topic (a Russian tax, then British ships), so nothing is ever set side by side. Both facts might be true, but neither one answers “similar” or “different.”
A BETTER ANSWER “In contrast to the Russian empire, which was a land-based empire, the British empire was a maritime-based empire. They were similar, however, in that their expansion led indigenous peoples to become exposed to diseases (like smallpox), which ended up leading to a lot of deaths for the indigenous peoples.”
Why it’s better: each point names both empires and says what the same feature was for each (land-based vs. maritime; disease in both). It uses signal phrases like “in contrast” and “similar, however” so the reader can see the comparison. A 2-point question like this one is worth one contrast plus one similarity.
The key ideas under each comparison question are organized the same way: grouped as similarities and differences, with both sides of each comparison written out together.
Heads-up on Question 7: “In what ways were the Ottoman, Mughal, and Ming empires and dynasties tolerant and intolerant?” is essentially asking you to process and remember the Chua reading (“Tolerance and Intolerance in the East,” from Amy Chua’s Day of Empire). You may want to review it before you answer, and look for one tolerant and one intolerant example for each empire.

SAQ Practice

These practice sets are modeled on AP short-answer questions: a short primary or secondary source with an attribution, then parts a), b), and c) that ask you to identify, explain, and connect the source to other developments. On the test you'll get two sets of three parts, 2 points each, with partial credit. Write your own answer in complete sentences first; once you've written enough, a button lets you reveal full sample answers so you can compare. Those samples are written by AI and may not actually earn credit, so check with your teacher if you’re unsure.
